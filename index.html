A wedding planner spreadsheet for cross-cultural weddings: two families, two languages, two sets of customs, one plan

This is a planned spreadsheet (Excel + Google Sheets) for couples planning a bilingual, cross-cultural wedding — for example an English–Chinese wedding. Generic wedding planners assume one language and one set of customs. Cross-cultural couples end up adapting them by hand: guest names kept in two scripts, a tea ceremony squeezed into a timeline built only for a Western ceremony, red envelopes tracked in a separate notebook from the gift registry, and a budget split across two currencies. This planner, if built, would put those pieces in one place from the start: a bilingual, mail-merge-ready guest list and seating chart, both ceremony timelines in a single countdown plan, red-envelope and gift-registry tracking side by side, and a dual-currency budget and vendor tracker. It has not been built. This page answers one question only: is that specific angle worth building, at an intended price of $29.99 one-time?

Why validate before building

  • Wedding planner spreadsheets are an established paid category on Etsy: multiple sellers offer multi-tab planners (budget, guests, seating, vendors, timelines) in Excel and Google Sheets. The category sells. What is not established is whether the bilingual / cross-cultural angle matters enough for anyone to choose it over a generic template — that is the untested hypothesis this page exists to test.
  • Free wedding checklists and generic templates are everywhere, and an AI can generate a planning checklist on request. A static checklist is not the product. The only reason to pay would be the cross-cultural structure already worked out: two languages, both customs, both gift conventions, two currencies. When you sign up, please say plainly whether that structure is the part you actually need.
  • If signups show the bilingual angle does not resonate — if a generic planner plus your own notes already covers you — this idea gets dropped, and that is a successful test result, not a failure.

Planned modules (not built yet)

  • Bilingual guest list: names in both languages/scripts, household grouping, RSVP status, mail-merge-ready for invitations
  • Bilingual seating chart: both families' tables in one view, with the fields you need for place cards
  • Combined timeline: tea ceremony and other Chinese customs alongside the Western ceremony and reception, in one countdown checklist
  • Customs checklist for both traditions: what each side expects, in the order it happens
  • Red-envelope ledger and gift registry, side by side: given and received, so the books balance after the wedding
  • Dual-currency budget and vendor tracker: deposits paid, balances due and their dates, in both currencies

What actually gets built — and which modules come first — will be decided by what signups say. If the cross-cultural modules are not what people ask for, the honest conclusion is that a generic planner already does the job.

Who this is for

  • Couples planning a wedding across two languages or two cultures, already keeping parallel lists to make a generic planner fit
  • Couples combining a Chinese tea ceremony (or similar customs) with a Western ceremony and reception
  • If a generic wedding planner already fits your wedding, you do not need this — use the generic one

FAQ

Which language pairs?
The planned first version is English–Chinese, because that is the combination the planned customs modules (tea ceremony, red envelopes) belong to. If your wedding combines a different pair, say so in your signup email — that is exactly the signal this test is collecting.
